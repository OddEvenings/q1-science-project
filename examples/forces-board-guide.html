Left panel your Introduction and Body

What Is a Force?

What goes here

The meaning of a force, and what forces can do.

How to make it
  • Write the meaning in big letters: a push or a pull.
  • Add one short sentence about what forces can do.

From your writing (Introduction): your Introduction.

Balanced Forces

What goes here

What balanced forces are and what happens to the object.

How to make it
  • Draw your object with 2 arrows that are the same length.
  • Write 2 short sentences under the picture.

Example: Same size pushes. The object stays still.

From your writing (Body): Body paragraph 1.

Unbalanced Forces

What goes here

What unbalanced forces are and which way the object moves.

How to make it
  • Draw your object with one long arrow and one short arrow.
  • Write 2 short sentences under the picture.

Example: One push is stronger. The object moves in the direction of the stronger push.

From your writing (Body): Body paragraph 2.

Center panel your Body paragraphs

Title

What goes here

A catchy title, a smaller subtitle, and your names.

How to make it
  • Put it at the top center, with the biggest letters on the board.
  • The subtitle should say balanced and unbalanced forces.

Example: Push and Pull: Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

From your writing (Whole paper): what your whole paper is about.

Our Model

What goes here

Two big pictures of your object: one with balanced forces and one with unbalanced forces.

A longer arrow means a stronger push or pull.
How to make it
  • Draw force arrows. A longer arrow means a stronger force.
  • Write the strength on each arrow, like 3 and 3, or 4 and 1.
  • Under each picture, write what happened: stayed still, or moved.

From your writing (Body): the model or test you described in Body paragraphs 1 and 2.

Science Words

What goes here

The words force, motion, balanced, and unbalanced, with short meanings.

How to make it
  • Make 4 small cards in a row under your model.
  • Write each word big, and the meaning small.

From your writing (Whole paper): look for the words you made bold in your paper.

Right panel your Body and Conclusion

Balanced or Unbalanced?

What goes here

3 or 4 real-life examples, each labeled balanced or unbalanced.

How to make it
  • Write one short sentence for each example.
  • You can hide the answer under a flap so visitors can guess.

Example: A book sits on a table. (balanced)

From your writing (Body): the everyday examples in your Body paragraphs.

What We Learned

What goes here

The two big rules you learned about forces.

How to make it
  • Write 2 sentences: one about balanced forces, and one about unbalanced forces.
  • Make this card stand out with a bright border.

Example: Balanced forces: the object stays still.

From your writing (Conclusion): your Conclusion.

How your board matches your writing

Your board and your paper tell the same story. Write your paper first. Then take the most important ideas from each paragraph, make them short, and put them on the matching panel.

Introduction → Left panel

What a force is. The balanced and unbalanced cards come from your Body.

Body paragraphs → Center panel

Your model with force arrows and numbers, and real-life examples.

Conclusion → Right panel

The two big rules you learned.

Keep it short. Use 1 or 2 sentences on the board for each paragraph in your paper.
Arrows matter. Make the arrow length match how strong the force is.
Big and neat. Visitors should read your title from far away.
Practice talking. Use your paper to explain the board in your own words.
