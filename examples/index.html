Energy Transformation Board Guide

Each card shows what goes in that spot on your tri-fold, how to make it, and which part of your writing it comes from. Check off each section when it is done.

Left panel mostly your Introduction

Our Question

What goes here

The one big question your project answers.

How to make it
  • Write it as one question that ends with a question mark.
  • Use big, dark letters so visitors see it first.

Example: How does a ___ change energy?

From your writing (Introduction): the first sentence of your Introduction.

What Is Energy?

What goes here

A simple meaning of energy and of energy transformation.

How to make it
  • Write 2 short sentences, not a paragraph.
  • Make the science words bold or a different color.

From your writing (Introduction): the sentences in your Introduction that explain energy.

Forms of Energy

What goes here

The forms of energy in your object, like electrical, heat, light, sound, or motion.

How to make it
  • Give each form a small symbol and one line.
  • Pick one color for each form and use it everywhere on your board.

From your writing (Introduction): the part of your Introduction where you name the forms of energy.

Our Prediction

What goes here

What you thought would happen before you studied it.

How to make it
  • Write 1 or 2 sentences.
  • Keep it the same as your paper, even if it turned out wrong.

Example: We think the ___ changes ___ energy into ___ energy.

From your writing (Introduction): the last sentences of your Introduction.

Science Words

What goes here

3 or 4 important words and what they mean.

How to make it
  • Use the words you made bold in your paper.
  • Write each word big, then a short meaning after it.

From your writing (Whole paper): look through your whole paper for your science words.

Center panel mostly your Body paragraphs

Title

What goes here

A catchy title, a smaller subtitle that names your object, and your names.

How to make it
  • Put it at the top center, with the biggest letters on the board.
  • Print it or cut out letters. Make sure it is straight.

Example: From Plug to Pot: The Energy Transformation of a ___

From your writing (Whole paper): what your whole paper is about.

Energy Chain

What goes here

A quick summary: the energy that goes in, an arrow, then the energy that comes out.

How to make it
  • Use colored boxes with arrows between them.
  • Use the same colors as your Forms of Energy card.

Example: Electrical → Heat + Light

From your writing (Body): the main idea of your Body paragraphs, in a few words.

Diagram of Your Object

What goes here

A big drawing of your object that shows how the energy moves.

Draw this big. Arrows show where energy goes in and where it comes out.
How to make it
  • Draw it large in the middle of the board.
  • Label the parts. Add arrows for energy in and energy out.

From your writing (Body): Body paragraph 2, about how your object changes the energy.

Follow the Energy

What goes here

3 to 5 numbered steps that tell where the energy goes, in order.

How to make it
  • Number each step. Write one short sentence per step.
  • Put the steps in a row under your diagram, with arrows between them.

From your writing (Body): turn each sentence in your Body paragraphs into one short step. Body 1 gives the first steps (where energy comes from). Body 2 gives the last steps (how it changes).

Right panel mostly your Conclusion

Look Closer

What goes here

A close-up picture of the part where the energy changes.

How to make it
  • Draw or print one zoomed-in picture.
  • Add a label or a one-line caption.

Example: the coil of a stove, the bulb of a lamp, the motor of a fan

From your writing (Body): the most important sentence in Body paragraph 2.

Our Conclusion

What goes here

Was your prediction correct? What did you learn?

How to make it
  • Write 2 or 3 short sentences.
  • Start by saying if your prediction was correct.

Example: Our prediction was correct. The ___ changed ___ energy into ___ energy.

From your writing (Conclusion): the first sentences of your Conclusion.

Energy Is Never Used Up

What goes here

Where all of the energy goes. Energy only changes form.

How to make it
  • Write 2 sentences.
  • You can add a small arrow drawing showing where the extra energy goes.

From your writing (Conclusion): the part of your Conclusion about energy changing form.

Stay Safe!

What goes here

Safety tips for using your object.

How to make it
  • Write 2 or 3 short tips.
  • Use a red or orange card so it stands out.

From your writing (Conclusion): the safety sentences at the end of your Conclusion.

Check Yourself

What goes here

2 or 3 questions that visitors can try to answer.

How to make it
  • Write the answers under a flap, or on the back.
  • Use questions you can answer out loud at the science fair.

From your writing (Conclusion): make questions from the most important facts in your Conclusion.

How your board matches your writing

Your board and your paper tell the same story. Write your paper first. Then take the most important ideas from each paragraph, make them short, and put them on the matching panel.

Introduction → Left panel

Your question, what energy is, the forms of energy, and your prediction.

Body paragraphs → Center panel

Your energy chain, a big labeled diagram, and the steps that follow the energy.

Conclusion → Right panel

Whether your prediction was correct, where the energy goes, safety, and questions.

Keep it short. The board uses 1 or 2 sentences where your paper uses a whole paragraph.
Big and neat. Visitors should read your title and question from far away.
Same colors. Use one color for each form of energy, all over the board.
Practice talking. Use your paper to explain the board in your own words.
